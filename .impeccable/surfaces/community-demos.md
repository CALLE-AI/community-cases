# Four community demonstrations

## Direction contract
THESIS: Make each product's decision and resulting artifact visible and operable inside a native spatial scene. Conversation-Clarify, Care Call AI and HungryCall now follow the accepted RingBack baseline, with their own workflows and choreography. Local implementation and verification do not imply user acceptance or deployment.
OWN-WORLD: Preserve the matte miniature, warm white page, yellow/blue accents, system lettering and native HTML labels. The existing static site and vendored Three.js remain; assets/scene-kit.mjs shares RingBack's characters, phone rig, scene primitives and renderer across the four interactive cases.
STORY: Clarify an email through an approved question and reviewed draft; combine care requests into a reviewed handoff; stop a waitlist after acceptance and save only an available slot; stop restaurant calls at a delivery/budget match.
FIRST VIEWPORT: Existing identity and author links, then a full-width native scene with readable captions and compact controls. All four cases combine direct choices with idle automatic progression, with no separate player or mock. The three revised cases place captions in normal document flow above the canvas and use email, clipboard or receipt panels; at 900px and below, a 360px canvas sits above the controls. Preserve original source and evidence notes.
FORM: Extension of the accepted RingBack interaction and material baseline; no new visual-world selection. Distinct physical environments and actions per case. Fictional data, no calls or external sends. Care Call AI uses independently authored concept code, never copied proprietary assets. Local Atlas retains its original narrated player and separate public-facts mock.
FINISH: Follow DESIGN.md interaction-scene acceptance criteria: desktop/narrow browser inspection, state and motion tests, matched frame-time measurement and source/claim checks. The three revised cases retain Microsoft Jenny audio as optional standalone overviews with native audio controls and scripts, not synchronization for interactive branches. Record untested devices before deployment.

## Motion plan
Focal moments: an office desk leads to the recipient, then a supported answer returns into an unsent draft; an unclear answer leaves no draft. Cleaning and grocery requests from two same-day calls occupy separate clipboard slots before the coordinator handoff; an unclear callback retains cleaning and flags follow-up. A visible waiting queue declines/accepts before the accepted contact fills the diary. A restaurant street search tests price and delivery in order, stops at the first match, or exhausts all four options; no order is placed.
Continuity: each story has its own physical layout and choreography evaluated from workflow state and phase age. Handsets follow the shared grip rig, moving records use clearance routes, and cameras follow the active call or transferring record. Captions stay outside the canvas. Responsive shots preserve the active subject rather than squeezing a whole neighborhood into portrait width. Reconstructing the same state and phase age produces the same pose; reset starts the workflow again.
Feedback: native controls expose approvals, uncertainty and stop conditions. Manual choices and idle defaults share the same transitions. Real input resets only the idle clock; Pause auto stops automatic progression while visible scene motion continues. Offscreen and hidden pages stop animation and progression. CALL-E branding and simulated call counts remain in the native controls.
Budget: one shared renderer per page, native DPR, no continuous scene work offscreen; explicit audio and reduced-motion settled poses with auto initially paused. Keyboard controls remain operable in WebGL fallback. Reuse existing assets and modules, with no new runtime dependency.
